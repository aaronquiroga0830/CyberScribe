EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
------------------

We evaluated three Ollama chat models—phi3, llama3.2:3b, and gemma2:2b—on
identical mission-scoped Risk Management Plan (RMP) structured-edit jobs through
the production pipeline orchestrator (in-process, no HTTP). Each trial used
mission llm_benchmark over source documents in sample_mission, Ollama embeddings
(nomic-embed-text), and update intent full_refresh. RMP updates did not trigger
co-generation of Timeline reports. Trials were executed sequentially on a 16 GB
RAM CPU workstation with local Ollama.

Subsequent benchmark runs enforce a 5-minute wall-clock cap per trial and a
3-minute cap per Ollama generate call (structured edit or fallback draft) so
outlier jobs cannot run 20+ minutes. The canonical Experiment 2 run predates
those caps; interpret long llama trials (e.g. trial 7) as uncapped outliers.

We report 10 independent trials per model (canonical run). Phase timings
(retrieval, structured-edit generation, parse/validate) were taken from pipeline
job progress events. Structured success required at least one accepted block edit
with no JSON parse failure and no structured-edit fallback. Factual grounding was
scored by a local LLM-as-judge (llama3.2:3b, with cross-model assignment when
the subject model was Llama).


STRUCTURED-EDIT RELIABILITY
---------------------------

Figure [Y] (fig_paper_structured_reliability.pdf) reports structured success rate
and fallback rate across all 10 trials per model. Structured success was 40% for
gemma2:2b, 30% for llama3.2:3b, and 30% for phi3; fallback to full-draft RAG
occurred on 60–70% of trials for each model. Reliability should be read before
latency: most trials did not complete the intended structured-edit path.

Figure [Y] caption — see output/benchmark/experiment2/fig_paper_structured_reliability_caption.txt


PIPELINE LATENCY (STRUCTURED-SUCCESS TRIALS)
--------------------------------------------

Figure [X] (fig_paper_pipeline_phases.pdf) summarizes phase latency. Panel (A)
shows structured-edit generation time only among trials with structured success
(phi3 n=3, llama3.2:3b n=3, gemma2:2b n=4); whiskers are min–max within that
subset. On those trials, gemma2:2b and llama3.2:3b completed generation in
roughly 8–18 s; phi3 required roughly 72–107 s. Panel (B) shows retrieval and
parse/validate overhead (tens of milliseconds for all models). Uncapped outlier
trials (e.g. long fallback wall times) are excluded from panel (A) by design.

Results paragraph (canonical n = 10):

  Structured success rates were 40% (gemma2:2b), 30% (llama3.2:3b), and 30%
  (phi3), with fallback on 60–70% of trials. Among structured-success trials,
  mean generation time was 8.7 s (gemma2:2b), 12.6 s (llama3.2:3b), and

PILOT RUN (n = 2, superseded by n = 10 study)
---------------------------------------------

Early pilot data (Experiment 2, two trials per model) validated the harness before
the canonical 10-trial run. These values are illustrative only.

  Model          Mean gen (s)   Struct success   Fallback   Mean grounding
  -------------  -------------  ---------------  ---------  ----------------
  llama3.2:3b    8.6            100%             0%         4.38
  gemma2:2b      12.1           50%              50%        4.00
  phi3           117.2          0%               100%       4.00

On the pilot data, llama3.2:3b was the only model with perfect structured success
and sub-10 s mean generation latency. phi3 exhibited high generation latency and
fell back to non-structured output in both trials despite judge-reported grounding
scores of 4.0 on fallback edits. gemma2:2b showed bimodal behavior (one slow,
fallback trial; one fast, successful trial). The 10-trial canonical run may differ,
especially for phi3 (early canonical trials showed structured success).


MITIGATION OF HIGH FALLBACK RATES (FOLLOW-UP)
---------------------------------------------

The canonical run exposed brittle structured JSON output (invalid escapes in
HTML fields, array-vs-object format). We document this openly in the paper and
implemented mitigations in the product pipeline: Ollama format=json, {"edits":[]}
wrapper, server-side old_html from block IDs, robust parsing, and one structured
retry before full-draft fallback. See docs/paper/STRUCTURED_EDIT_RELIABILITY.md.
A re-benchmark (e.g. experiment2_json_mode) should report whether fallback rates
drop; the uncapped experiment2 figures remain the honest baseline.


INTERPRETATION AND LIMITATIONS
------------------------------

Results characterize this hardware, mission corpus, and prompt path only; they
do not establish statistical significance without sufficient trial count and
formal testing. Judge scores measure agreement with retrieved source text under a
rubric, not human expert adjudication. We do not claim superiority of one model
beyond the reported pipeline reliability and latency trade-offs on the benchmark
mission. Retrieval for RMP used a fixed chunk list (crew_log, findings, other),
not semantic top-k search; generation time therefore reflects structured-edit
LLM latency under full-context retrieval.
