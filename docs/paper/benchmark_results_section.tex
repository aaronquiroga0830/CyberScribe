\subsection{Local LLM Benchmark for RMP Structured Updates}
\label{sec:benchmark-rmp}

\paragraph{Experimental setup.}
We evaluated three Ollama chat models---\texttt{phi3}, \texttt{llama3.2:3b}, and \texttt{gemma2:2b}---on identical mission-scoped Risk Management Plan (RMP) structured-edit jobs through the production pipeline orchestrator (in-process, no HTTP). Each trial used mission \texttt{llm\_benchmark} over source documents in \texttt{sample\_mission}, Ollama embeddings (\texttt{nomic-embed-text}), and update intent \texttt{full\_refresh}. RMP updates did not trigger co-generation of Timeline reports. Trials were executed sequentially on a 16\,GB RAM CPU workstation with local Ollama.

We report \textbf{\BenchTrialN{} independent trials per model} (canonical run; see Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-pilot} for preliminary $n{=}2$ pilot values used while scaling trial count). Phase timings (retrieval, structured-edit generation, parse/validate) were taken from pipeline job progress events. Structured success required at least one accepted block edit with no JSON parse failure and no structured-edit fallback. Factual grounding was scored by a local LLM-as-judge (\texttt{llama3.2:3b}, with cross-model assignment when the subject model was Llama).

\paragraph{Pipeline latency.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline-phases} summarizes mean phase latency. Structured-edit \emph{generation} dominated end-to-end cost for all models. Retrieval loaded fixed mission chunk lists for RMP (crew logs, findings, and other document types) in tens of milliseconds; parse/validate added negligible overhead compared with generation.

On the pilot data, \texttt{llama3.2:3b} was the only model with perfect structured success and sub-10\,s mean generation latency. \texttt{phi3} exhibited high generation latency and fell back to non-structured output in both trials despite judge-reported grounding scores of 4.0 on fallback edits. \texttt{gemma2:2b} showed bimodal behavior (one slow, fallback trial; one fast, successful trial).

\paragraph{Interpretation and limitations.}
Results characterize this hardware, mission corpus, and prompt path only; they do not establish statistical significance at small $n$. Judge scores measure agreement with retrieved source text under a rubric, not human expert adjudication. We do not claim superiority of one model beyond the reported pipeline reliability and latency trade-offs on the benchmark mission.
